READERCOMPANION

Privacy Policy

Last updated: 21 November 2026

Overview

readerCompanion is a local-first desktop application for managing a personal e-book library on Windows and Linux. Its core features work without a Google account.

Information the application processes

The application processes book files and metadata you choose to import or scan, including titles, authors, reading status, ratings, and notes. Its library database is stored locally on your computer.

If you enable optional integrations, it also processes the information you choose to import from a Goodreads export or access from Google Drive.

Google Drive access

Connecting Google Drive is optional. When you authorize Google Drive, readerCompanion uses the requested drive.file permission to access files you select or open with the application. It can read selected e-book files to index their metadata and, when you explicitly request file-management operations, rename, move, or organize those files.

The application stores authorization information on your device to maintain the connection. Disconnecting Google Drive stops the application's use of that connection; you can also revoke its access in your Google Account settings.

Storage and sharing

readerCompanion is designed to store its library catalog in a local SQLite database. Google Drive files remain in your Google Drive unless you explicitly perform a file-management action. Optional cover lookups may contact Open Library when you enable and use that feature.

This policy does not claim that all application activity is exclusively local: Google Drive requests necessarily communicate with Google's services, and optional online features communicate with their respective providers.

Your choices

You can use readerCompanion without Google Drive, disconnect a linked Google account, and manage or back up your local library data through the application's available features.

Contact

For privacy questions, please use the project's GitHub issue tracker. Before publishing, the developer should verify that this contact method and all data-handling statements accurately describe the released application.
